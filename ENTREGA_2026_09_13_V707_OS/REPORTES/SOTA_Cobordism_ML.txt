SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION TO TOPOLOGICAL QUANTUM FIELD THEORIES IN DEEP LEARNING
Traditional deep learning models view neural networks as parameterized compositions of functions acting on vector spaces or statistical manifolds. However, as networks grow in depth and dimensionality, tracking local statistical correlations via gradient descent becomes prone to vanishing gradients, adversarial vulnerabilities, and geometric distortions. By shifting the paradigm from differential geometry to algebraic topology, we can model neural networks as Topological Quantum Field Theories (TQFTs).
In an n-dimensional TQFT, spacetime is modeled as an n-dimensional cobordism between (n-1)-dimensional manifolds. By analogy, a neural network layer can be conceptualized not merely as a point-wise mapping between features, but as a physical cobordism - a topological space that continuously deforms the input manifold (initial state) into the output manifold (final state).

SECTION 2: NEURAL NETWORK LAYERS AS COBORDISMS
A cobordism between two closed (n-1)-dimensional manifolds M and N is an n-dimensional manifold W whose boundary is the disjoint union of M and N.
In the context of machine learning, an input layer represents the manifold M, reflecting the data topology in latent space. The subsequent layer represents N, the transformed latent space. The computation performed by the layer, conventionally a matrix multiplication followed by non-linearity, acts as the cobordism W bridging M to N.
Instead of treating the weights as statistical variables, they define the metric and topological structure of W. If two neural architectures or weight configurations are topologically equivalent (i.e., they are diffeomorphic cobordisms), they compute the same global topological invariant, making the learning process robust against local, adversarial perturbations.

SECTION 3: THE COBORDISM HYPOTHESIS AND LATENT REPRESENTATIONS
The Cobordism Hypothesis (proposed by Baez and Dolan, formalized by Lurie) states that a fully extended TQFT is uniquely determined by its value on a single point, known as the fully dualizable object.
Applying this to Machine Learning: if a neural network acts as a fully extended TQFT, the global representation learned by the network over the entire data manifold is completely determined by how the network locally transforms a single fully dualizable input point across its layers.
This provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for why certain local learning algorithms, like backpropagation on single batches, can successfully generalize to global data manifolds. The network is essentially learning an invariant functor from the category of cobordisms (the architectures) to the category of vector spaces (the latent representations).
